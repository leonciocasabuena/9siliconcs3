## Sari-Sari Store Inventory System

### - Encapsulation
#### This concept/pillar may help by encapsulating and grouping every variable and method that define and categorize a certain object (e.g. a store product) from the others, which would then be group into one single entity late on and act as an entire category working together (e.g. properties like price, name, and quantity will embody certain store product.) Applying this concept further eradicates the problem with creating too many variables that are too inefficient to store and organize easily.

### - Abstraction
#### This concept will absolutely come in handy if the vendor wants to eliminate any redundant information with regard to his or her products and merchandise and to present the most relevant and essential properties of the products itself (e.g. product name, price, expiration date, quantity, etc.). The application of this pillar will significantly reduce the workload of the vendor all while  omitting superfluous properties.

### - Inheritance
#### This pillar may now serve as a stepping stone or bridge in order to sort products that were already encapsulated early on; the inheritance pillar is essential for grouping and classifying products that lie in the same category of product type (e.g. edible products categorized as foods, cleaning materials categorized as hygiene, etc.). Applying this concept will further ameliorate the struggle of trying to effectively and efficiently sort products however the vendor desires.

### - Polymorphism
#### Lastly, the Polymorphism concept may work best in calculating different aspects where numbers overlap (e.g. budgeting, change calculation, profit calculation, etc.), for a simple calculator inherently represents the properties of polymorphism———as it can actively "morph" and perform different tasks the vendor desires (addition, subtraction, etc.). 

## Reflection
### In my opinion, the Encapsulation pillar may be the best fit for an aspiring sari-sari vendor to use, because encapsulating and merging different properties at once is far optimal for it bundles a product's details and actions into one neat package, preventing errors and keeping things organized just like sorting items into specific, labeled containers on a shelf.
